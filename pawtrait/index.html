Pawtrait

A favorite photo of your pet, turned into a portrait and a phrase that sounds like them. Three working prototypes, each with a different job: the shippable product, the human check behind it, and the full vision it's heading toward.

Prototypes

Phase 1 — The shippable version

Current

Started narrow — will anyone pay $29 for one thing? — and became the opposite of a vision demo: scoped to what's buildable cheap and manual-first, with no automation pretending to be more capable than it is. One product (the pillow), real compositing against an actual product photo, a hardwired phrase library, and an honest placeholder anywhere a real asset doesn't exist yet. The flow — Photo, Phrase, Choose & Approve, About, Review, Checkout — is built around ecommerce instincts.

Approach: restraint. Test real purchase behavior on one real thing; nothing built ahead of proof it's wanted.

Review queue — Human oversight

Internal tool

Not customer-facing. It proves out the one principle that mattered most: nothing AI-generated reaches a customer without a human checking it first. Two queues (Creative Brief Approval and Proof QC), structured flags instead of free-text notes, and a tally that shows when a gate is catching enough real problems to justify still existing.

Approach: make governance demonstrable, not theoretical — a reviewer can click through it, not just read the policy. Open it beside Phase 1 in the same browser; approvals update the customer tab within seconds.

Concept — The full vision

North star

What the finished product looks like at full ambition — no real users, no cost constraints. Hero, upload, a chapter arc (gotcha day, favorites, milestones), style selection, render, proof and buy, then a rich Memory Page: multi-photo stacks, photo-date and location detection, and a real timeline. Sage and Lou and all.

Approach: aspirational completeness — the whole lifetime pet-memory platform in one place, something to point at and say "this is where we're going."
